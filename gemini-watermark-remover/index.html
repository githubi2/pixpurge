The Gemini Badge

What the Gemini Watermark Looks Like on an Image

Spot it once and you'll spot it every time — it's the same little mark across Google's image tools.

It's small, easy to miss, and sitting in a corner of the frame: a four-point sparkle, white or light gray, resting over whatever happens to be underneath. If your picture came from the Gemini app, Imagen, or AI Studio, that sparkle is most likely the watermark you're staring at.

Because the badge sits on top of the photo rather than inside it, it can be cleaned off without leaving a hole — as long as the pixels underneath get rebuilt instead of blurred. That rebuild is the whole job here: the badge goes, and the corner keeps its texture.

What's in the File

What the Gemini Watermark Is — and What Stays in the File

Google marks the images its AI tools produce so it's possible to tell where they came from, and the sparkle badge is only the visible part of that system. It's the part this page is about: the mark you can see on a Gemini, Imagen, or AI Studio image, cleared away with the corner behind it rebuilt.

One honest note. AI images can also carry information written into the pixels themselves, and that part isn't something this cleanup touches — it stays in the file after the badge is gone. Better to say that plainly now than have you find out later.

And if the picture you're cleaning up isn't from a Google tool, that's fine too — the same engine handles logos, stamps, and date labels. For the broader walkthrough, head over to remove a watermark from any image.
