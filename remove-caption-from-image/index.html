Captions vs subtitles vs stickers

Three Things People Call "a Caption"

They are handled differently, mostly because they are not the same kind of object.

Captions

Text laid over a picture: quote cards, meme lines, a description under a photo, a repost credit. Letters over pixels, so detection picks them up reliably.

Best case for the one-click button.

Subtitles

Caption lines for video — burned into the frame, or sitting in a still you grabbed as a thumbnail. Once they are pixels, they are treated exactly like a caption: found, cleared, filled.

If the subtitle is a separate track in a video file, there is nothing to erase from the picture — that is a subtitle editor's job.

Stickers and emoji

Graphic overlays rather than type: an emoji, a GIF sticker, a drawn arrow. Text detection may ignore them because there is no text to read.

Mark them with the brush or the box tool instead — same removal, more control.

Reposts, screenshots and thumbnails

Three situations cover most of the traffic on this page. Someone reposts a photo and the old account handle is still baked into the corner. Someone needs a screenshot for a deck and the chat or the UI gets in the way. Someone makes a thumbnail from a frame that still has the show's subtitle in it.

All three are pixel problems with a pixel fix. What they are not is a licence: removing a caption does not change who owns the picture. If the words you are removing are someone's attribution, taking them off and passing the image on is a bad idea — legally and otherwise.

The practical detail that trips people up: captions often sit at the very edge of the frame, and the fill needs room to work. Leave the full frame in, remove the words, and crop afterwards if you want the tighter composition.
