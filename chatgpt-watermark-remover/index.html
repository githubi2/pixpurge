ChatGPT Watermark Remover

Erase the visible ChatGPT badge from your own images — and understand what is left behind in the file's metadata, which no pixel editor can touch.

What you are looking at

What the ChatGPT Badge Actually Looks Like

It depends on where the image came from, and that is the first thing worth checking before you try to remove anything.

A downloaded file often has no visible mark at all

Plenty of ChatGPT image exports come out clean: no corner logo, no caption, nothing in the pixels. If you cannot see a badge on the image, there is nothing for a pixel editor to remove — the mark that is present in those files is metadata, and that is a different problem.

A small chip or wordmark in a corner

In-app views and some exports show a small logo or a line of type in the bottom corner. Translucent marks read as a soft grey patch on light backgrounds and disappear on dark ones. This is the case PixPurge handles: it is a graphic in the image, so it comes off like any other graphic.

A screenshot of the chat window

Screenshots bring their own clutter — the surrounding interface, a caption line, sometimes a filename. All of it is text and UI over pixels, so one pass usually clears the lot; brush anything you want to keep.

Two different mechanisms

Visible Badge vs Invisible Provenance

Worth being blunt about this, because a lot of pages about "ChatGPT watermark removal" quietly imply they can erase both.

Visible badge — yes

A mark you can point at: a logo, a wordmark, a "Generated with" line sitting in the corner of the picture. It is ordinary pixels, so it can be erased and the corner rebuilt.

What PixPurge does: detects the glyphs, removes them, fills the area from the surrounding image.

Invisible provenance — no

C2PA manifests and similar signing schemes live in the file's metadata; some generators also embed imperceptible signals in the pixel data. Neither is a badge, and neither is removed by editing the picture.

What PixPurge does: nothing, on purpose. We do not claim to strip provenance, and you should be sceptical of anything that does.

So: cleaning the badge gives you a picture that looks clean. It does not make the file read as camera-captured, and it does not change what the file says about its own origin.
